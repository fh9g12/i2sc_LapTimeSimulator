# Group report brief

As a team, you are also tasked with writing a technical memorandum
(report) of your design process, alongside your parameter submission.
**Maximum 10 pages.**

This report is as much about *practising the structure of a technical
report* as it is about your specific results — the sections below map
onto the standard structure you'll be expected to use again in later
coursework and lab reports.

## Structure

**Title**
Your team name/number and a descriptive title.

**Abstract (Executive Summary)**
A few sentences summarising the whole report: what you did, how you
approached it, and what you ended up choosing — written so someone who
reads *only* this section still knows the headline outcome. Write this
last, even though it appears first.

**Introduction**
Briefly outline the task, assuming the reader is a competent engineer
who has never seen this exercise before: what the challenge was (choose
a front wing, rear wing and gear ratio to race over a full season),
what you were free to change, and how the competition is scored. Don't
assume the reader has read any of the project's own getting-started
guides.

**Methodology**
Briefly explain how the simulation tool works, enough for the reader to
trust your results without having to read the code:
- How your team estimated Cl, Cd and aero balance from a wing choice
  (what tool, what you swept, what you found achievable).
- How lap time was estimated from a Cl/Cd/aeroBalance/gear-ratio
  combination (what the simulation does, at a level of detail
  appropriate for an engineer who won't read the source).
- Any studies/DOE approach you used to search the parameter space.

**Results**
Present how you arrived at your final parameter choice: the studies you
ran, what they showed, and how they narrowed your options down. Use
plots and tables rather than prose to make your case. At the end of this
section, state your final chosen parameters and the performance
trade-offs you made across the season.

**Conclusions**
A short overview of what was achieved: your final parameter choice, how
you expect it to perform, and (briefly) what you'd try next with more
time.

## A few notes

- Figures and tables count toward making your case, not toward padding
  length — a report that hits 6 well-chosen pages is not at a
  disadvantage against one that hits 10.
- Figures should look professional and be readable (text shouldn't be
  too small), with the information of interest taking up most of the
  area (minimise unnecessary whitespace).
- Where you made an engineering trade-off (e.g. chose less peak
  downforce for a more consistent season), say so explicitly and justify
  it — that reasoning is worth more marks than the raw lap time.
- Cite the parameter values you actually submitted on the Teams form
  somewhere in the report (Results or an appendix) — a report whose
  numbers don't match the submission will be flagged.
